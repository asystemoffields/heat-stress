What this shows

WBGT (Wet-Bulb Globe Temperature) is the heat-stress measure used by militaries, athletics, and occupational-safety bodies, because it accounts for humidity, sun, and wind — not just air temperature. This tool estimates WBGT and the NWS Heat Index anywhere on Earth from a free weather forecast, and maps it to plain-language activity guidance.

  • WBGT (in sun) assumes direct sun on open ground; in shade is the lower value out of direct sun. Night hours are shown as the shade value.
  • Flags (White→Black) follow US military WBGT categories; the Heat Index uses NWS health categories.
  • Everything is computed in your browser. No login, nothing stored or sent anywhere.

Important: values are estimated from forecast data — the globe and longwave radiation terms are modeled, not measured — so treat them as planning guidance, not a measured WBGT or an official heat alert. The estimate is least reliable at night and under clear, calm skies, and over bright surfaces (snow, sand, fresh asphalt). Real heat stress also depends on shade, wind, hydration, clothing, acclimatization, age and health. When in doubt, consult your national weather service and local guidance.

WBGT model: Kong & Huber (2024), an analytic form of Liljegren et al. (2008).
